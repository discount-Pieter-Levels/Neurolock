Proof of personhood · Field Station

Prove you're one live human.
Without handing over your body.

NeuroLock turns the veins inside your finger into a cryptographic key, checks that a live person is present, and reads consent from your brain's response to light. The registry stores no images and no templates: it holds commitments and hashes only.

You'll go through the station yourself. The capture hardware isn't connected yet, so you load recorded finger-vein scans (FV-USM) and EEG (PhysioNet) by dragging them in. Everything after that is the real pipeline and its real outputs. The parts we can't measure yet are marked Simulated.

03 · One human, one identity

Has this finger enrolled before?

Before issuing an identity, the station compares the new veins against everyone already enrolled. A score above the duplicate line means "you're already in here". This blocks Sybil attacks, where one person makes many accounts.

Registry search REAL SCORES

Fused Gabor + max-curvature score: 2 fingers, 6 images each, z-scored against impostor statistics. Duplicate line = , chosen on held-out people.

Measured on the test half: 0 false duplicates, 98% of repeat enrolments caught. Caveat: 2 fingers is safe for about 1–10k people, not for a global registry.

04b · Liveness · Brain response

Look at the flicker. Your visual cortex echoes it.

When you look at a light flickering at f Hz, the back of your brain oscillates at f, 2f and 3f. That's SSVEP. The server picks a random sequence of frequencies. A live person watching produces EEG that follows the sequence, and a recording made earlier can't, because the sequence didn't exist yet.

Photosensitivity: this step flickers at 6–15 Hz.

05 · Key derivation

A secret that only your finger can unlock

The vein map becomes a 1530-bit code. We don't store it. We store it XORed with a random error-correcting codeword, which looks like noise. On a later visit, a fresh scan is never identical, so some bits flip. The BCH code fixes up to flips per 255-bit block. Your finger lands close enough to fix. Someone else's lands about 30% away and doesn't.

06 · Ledger

The ledger only ever sees hashes

Each record carries the SHA-256 of the one before it, so editing history breaks every later link. These hashes are computed live in your browser. Built for the Logos LEZ testnet (registry program written and tested; deploy waiting on the testnet, which halted).

07 · Consent

Say yes or no by looking

For high-stakes actions, CONFIRM and DENY both flicker. Every 1.5 s each one jumps to a new random frequency. Your EEG follows whichever one you're looking at, so the CONFIRM sequence is tied to this exact action and this one-time code. Coercing a click doesn't help; you have to look at it.

Where we are, honestly

Real: vein pipeline, matching, fuzzy commitment, dedup, ledger logic (Rust registry program, 14/14 tests). Simulated: SSVEP responses and finger pulse (no recorded datasets yet). Not built: NIR capture hardware. Known limit: anyone who controls the capture software can synthesise a pulse and an SSVEP. Stopping that needs trusted sensor hardware.
